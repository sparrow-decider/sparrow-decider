Which one?

  • Your input is sensor numbers and the questions never change → pico
  • Your input is text and the device is an ESP32-S3 → nano-general
  • Your input mixes text and sensor readings → nano-robot
  • You have a Pi, a laptop or a Jetson and want more headroom → micro, tiny or small

Model card

What it does
Answers noul (yes/no), choice (pick one) and score (rate on a scale) questions about a state, with calibrated probabilities, in the same shapes as Strands Decider.
Teachers
A full Strands Decider (4B) teaches the text tiers through distillation on ~430k decisions. pico models are labelled by the public Strands Decider 2B on sampled device states.
Base weights
micro and nano from potion-base-8M (MinishLab, MIT); tiny and small from Ettin encoders (JHU CLSP, MIT).
Runtimes
Python (numpy only); C99, no malloc, model read from flash (ESP-IDF component); sparrow-pico compiler: device spec → C header.
Calibration
Each model ships per-kind temperatures fitted on held-out data. A yes/no answer near 0.5 means "don't know".
Licence
Apache-2.0 (code); model weights derived from MIT-licensed potion-base-8M and Ettin encoders.

Limitations

  • Open-ended language it never trained on: well behind the 2B (MASSIVE domain routing 0.37-0.67 across released Sparrow models vs 0.86).
  • Yes/no judgement on JevBench is near chance at every tier.
  • pico answers only the questions it was compiled for.
  • These models suggest decisions. Don't use them as the only safety mechanism on a physical system.
